Help

How Genboard is organized, and how to use each part.

Projects and sessions

A project is one research effort, such as one family line. Inside it, a session is one sitting of research with a goal, like “Find Émilie's father”. Create a project with New Project at the bottom of the sidebar, then start a session from it. Click an earlier session in the sidebar to resume it; its tabs reopen.

Browsing

Use Browse (⌘1) to work in the built-in browser. The Sites menu and the start page open FamilySearch, WikiTree, Find a Grave and Généalogie Québec. Your logins are remembered between launches. Every page you open is recorded in the session's Timeline (right-hand panel).

  • Pin a page with ⌘D: Genboard saves a screenshot and any text you selected, and asks why the page matters.
  • Mark a dead end with ⇧⌘D when a page was checked and had nothing useful.
  • The Timeline shows pins and dead ends. Check Show all pages to see everything. Right-click a page to edit its note, transcribe it with AI, or open it in a new tab.

Ending a session

End Session (⇧⌘E) asks where you left off, your next steps (each becomes a to-do) and open questions, and whether to keep the open tabs. The same prompt appears when you switch sessions or quit. Next time, a Last time banner shows that note and the to-dos.

The research board

Use Board (⌘2). Each project has one board.

  • Add cards from + Add: Person, Event (baptism, marriage, burial, census…), Note, or Evidence from a pinned page. You can also drag a pinned page from the Timeline onto the board.
  • Edit a card by clicking it. Flag a card as a Question or a Conflict.
  • Connect two cards by dragging the round handle on a card's edge onto the other card, or select one and shift-click the other. In the card editor, set what the connection means: related, spouse of, parent of, child of, supports or contradicts. Click ✎ beside a connection to give it a label or a note.
  • Move cards by dragging them, pan by dragging the background, and zoom with the toolbar or a pinch.

Theories

Click Theories on the board toolbar. A theory is a statement you're testing, like “Émilie's father is Antoine Tremblay”. Press + to start one (it begins with the selected card), then click it to make it active: other cards dim, and the cards and connections you add go into it. A card can belong to several theories, so competing versions share the facts they agree on.

Set a theory's status to Open, Supported or Refuted. Its cards show a dashed border while it's open and a solid one once supported; cards that belong only to refuted theories are greyed out. Each theory lists its evidence for and against (from supports and contradicts connections) and has its own to-dos. Use the eye button to hide a theory.

Checks

Checks on the board toolbar compares the years on person and event cards along their connections, and shows a count when it finds something:

  • A burial or baptism that disagrees with a person's stated dates.
  • Someone appearing in an event after their death or before their birth.
  • A spouse under 13 at marriage, a parent under 13 or over 70 at a birth, or a child born after a parent died.
  • Two cards with the same name that might be one person.

Write dates on person cards as “1861–1934”, “b. 1826”, “d. 1888” or “b. ~1862”. Marriage checks apply to people connected to the marriage as a spouse (the spouse of role, or a label such as groom, bride, époux or épouse), since boards often connect children to their parents' marriage.

Family tree and GEDCOM

Use Tree (⌘3) for an ancestor or descendant chart built from the board's parent of, child of and spouse of connections. Pick the starting person from the menu, or double-click anyone in the chart. Competing parents from different theories are all shown in their theory colors.

GEDCOM in the tree toolbar saves a GEDCOM 5.5.1 file for FamilySearch, Ancestry, Gramps or MacFamilyTree: people, births, baptisms, deaths, burials, marriages, families, and your evidence cards as sources. Refuted and hidden theories are left out. Set each person's Sex in the card editor so parents are placed as husband and wife.

The Claude assistant

The AI features are optional and use your own Anthropic API key, added in Settings (⌘,) and stored in your Keychain. Usage is billed to your Anthropic account; the Assistant panel shows the running cost of each session.

  • Assistant (right-hand panel): ask questions. It sees your pins, notes, dead ends, to-dos, board, theories and earlier session summaries, plus the current page if you attach its text or a screenshot.
  • Transcribe with AI (right-click a pinned page): transcribes and translates a record and lists the people and events in it.
  • Suggest Cards with AI (on an evidence card): proposes people, events and relationships from the record. Nothing is added until you review the list and click Add.
  • Draft with AI (when ending a session): writes a first draft of your summary.

Choose the model for each feature in Settings. See the Privacy Policy for what is sent.

Importing a Scapple board

Click the import button on the board toolbar and choose a .scap file. Notes become note cards in the same places and colors, and lines and arrows become connections. Change a card's type afterwards in its editor.

Your data

Everything is stored on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/Genboard/ (the database and pinned screenshots). To back it up, quit Genboard and copy that folder.

Something wrong?

Report it on GitHub Issues, with your macOS version and what you were doing when it happened.
