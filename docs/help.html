Help

How Genboard is organized, and how to use each part.

Projects and sessions

A project is one research effort, such as one family line. Inside it, a session is one sitting of research with a goal, like “Find Émilie's father”. Create a project with New Project at the bottom of the sidebar, then start a session from it. Click an earlier session in the sidebar to resume it; its tabs reopen.

Browsing

Use Browse (⌘1) to work in the built-in browser. The Sites menu and the start page open FamilySearch, WikiTree, Find a Grave and Généalogie Québec. Your logins are remembered between launches. Every page you open is recorded in the session's Timeline (right-hand panel).

  • Pin a page with ⌘D: Genboard saves a screenshot and any text you selected, and asks why the page matters.
  • Mark a dead end with ⇧⌘D when a page was checked and had nothing useful.
  • The Timeline shows pins and dead ends. Check Show all pages to see everything. Right-click a page to edit its note, transcribe it with AI, or open it in a new tab.

Ending a session

End Session (⇧⌘E) asks where you left off, your next steps (each becomes a to-do) and open questions, and whether to keep the open tabs. The same prompt appears when you switch sessions or quit. Next time, a Last time banner shows that note and the to-dos.

The research board

Use Board (⌘2). Each project has one board.

  • Add cards from + Add: Person, Event (baptism, marriage, burial, census…), Note, or Evidence from a pinned page. You can also drag a pinned page from the Timeline onto the board.
  • Edit a card by clicking it. Flag a card as a Question or a Conflict.
  • Connect two cards by dragging the round handle on a card's edge onto the other card, or select one and shift-click the other.
  • Click a connection line (or its label) to edit it: what it means (related, spouse of, parent of, child of, supports or contradicts), its direction, a label for the line, a note on why it's there, and which theories it belongs to. The card editor lists a card's connections too.
  • Move cards by dragging them, pan by dragging the background, and zoom with the toolbar or a pinch.

Citations and source quality

Every evidence card has a Source section: a full citation (record, date, place, page or folio), the repository, and two ratings from the Genealogical Proof Standard:

  • Source: Original (the record itself or an image of it, like a Drouin parish register page), Derivative (an index, transcription or abstract) or Authored (someone's conclusions, like an online tree or a memorial).
  • Information: Primary (from someone who was there, like the priest at a baptism) or Secondary (heard from others, like an age at death).

Genboard guesses the source type from the site when you add a record, so a Drouin image starts as original and a WikiTree profile as authored. Change it whenever you know better. The ratings show on the card, weigh each theory's evidence, and go into the GEDCOM export and reports.

Theories

Click Theories on the board toolbar. A theory is a statement you're testing, like “Émilie's father is Antoine Tremblay”. Press + to start one (it begins with the selected card), then click it to make it active: other cards dim, and the cards and connections you add go into it. A card can belong to several theories, so competing versions share the facts they agree on.

Set a theory's status to Open, Supported or Refuted. Its cards show a dashed border while it's open and a solid one once supported; cards that belong only to refuted theories are greyed out. Each theory lists its evidence for and against (from supports and contradicts connections) and has its own to-dos. Use the eye button to hide a theory.

The bars beside each theory show the weight of its evidence: one original record with primary information counts fully, an online tree about a quarter, a note of your own a fifth. A theory reads Strong, Fair or Weak, or Contested when the evidence against comes close to the evidence for. It's a guide for where to look next, not a verdict.

Checks

Checks on the board toolbar compares the years on person and event cards along their connections, and shows a count when it finds something:

  • A burial or baptism that disagrees with a person's stated dates.
  • Someone appearing in an event after their death or before their birth.
  • A spouse under 13 at marriage, a parent under 13 or over 70 at a birth, or a child born after a parent died.
  • Two cards with the same name that might be one person.

Write dates on person cards as “1861–1934”, “b. 1826”, “d. 1888” or “b. ~1862”. Marriage checks apply to people connected to the marriage as a spouse (the spouse of role, or a label such as groom, bride, époux or épouse), since boards often connect children to their parents' marriage.

Family tree and GEDCOM

Use Tree (⌘3) for an ancestor or descendant chart built from the board's parent of, child of and spouse of connections. Pick the starting person from the menu, or double-click anyone in the chart. Competing parents from different theories are all shown in their theory colors.

GEDCOM in the tree toolbar saves a GEDCOM 5.5.1 file for FamilySearch, Ancestry, Gramps or MacFamilyTree: people, births, baptisms, deaths, burials, marriages, families, and your evidence cards as sources with their citations and quality ratings. Refuted and hidden theories are left out. Set each person's Sex in the card editor so parents are placed as husband and wife.

To start from an existing tree, choose File ▸ Import to Board… (⇧⌘I) and pick the .ged file, or drag it from the Finder onto the board. People arrive as person cards laid out as a tree, with parents above their children, couples side by side (marriage year on the line), and the full dates, places, notes and source titles in each card's notes.

Reports

Export Research Report (⇧⌘R, or right-click a project in the sidebar) writes up the whole project or a single theory for family or a genealogical society: each theory's status and weight of evidence, the evidence for and against with citations, connection notes, open questions, people, sources, and the research log of sessions and pinned pages. Save it as a PDF, Markdown or a web page.

Search

Search All Research (⇧⌘F, or the magnifying glass at the bottom of the sidebar) looks through every project: pages and their notes, transcriptions, cards, citations, connection notes, theories, session summaries and to-dos. Case and accents don't matter, so “helene” finds Hélène. Every word must appear. Use the arrow keys and Return to open a result where it lives; a result in another project opens that project's latest session.

The AI assistant

The AI features are optional. They use Claude with your own Anthropic API key, added in Settings (⌘,) and stored in your Keychain, or a model running on your own Mac. Claude usage is billed to your Anthropic account; the Assistant panel shows the running cost of each session.

  • Assistant (right-hand panel): ask questions. It sees your pins, notes, dead ends, to-dos, board, theories and earlier session summaries, plus the current page if you attach its text or a screenshot.
  • Transcribe with AI (right-click a pinned page): transcribes and translates a record and lists the people and events in it.
  • Suggest Cards with AI (on an evidence card): proposes people, events and relationships from the record. Nothing is added until you review the list and click Add.
  • Draft with AI (when ending a session): writes a first draft of your summary.

Choose the model for each feature in Settings. See the Privacy Policy for what is sent.

A local model

Questions and session summaries can run on a free model on your Mac with MLX, so nothing leaves the computer. In Terminal (the first run downloads about 17 GB; a Mac with 32 GB of memory or more is best):

uv tool install mlx-lm
mlx_lm.server --model mlx-community/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507-4bit

Then in Settings, under Local model (MLX), press Test Connection and choose Local model (MLX) for a task. LM Studio and Ollama work too; enter their address. Local models read text only, so keep Claude for transcribing handwritten records.

Importing a Scapple board

Choose File ▸ Import to Board… (⇧⌘I, or the import button on the board toolbar) and pick a .scap file, or drag the file from the Finder onto the board. Notes become note cards in the same places and colors, and lines and arrows become connections. Change a card's type afterwards in its editor.

Backups and your data

Everything is stored on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/Genboard/ (the database and pinned screenshots).

In the File menu, Export Project… saves the current project, and Back Up All Projects… saves every project, as one .genboard file with its sessions, pages, screenshots, board, theories and to-dos. Restore from Backup… adds the projects in such a file next to yours; it never replaces anything, and a project whose name is taken comes back as “(restored)”. Use it to keep a copy somewhere safe or to move your research to another Mac.

Updates

The downloaded app checks for updates once a day and asks before installing one; Genboard ▸ Check for Updates… checks now. With Homebrew, the app updates itself the same way.

Something wrong?

Report it on GitHub Issues, with your macOS version and what you were doing when it happened.
